### Technical Report: Modern Short Side & Blind Side Attacking Strategies

#### 1\. OVERVIEW

As modern Rush and Drift defensive systems reach peak sophistication, the short side and blind side have evolved into primary strike zones for high-value yardage and try-scoring opportunities. These channels are specifically engineered to exploit "honeypotting" at the pillar and post, where defenders over-congest the open side and leave the opposite fringe vulnerable. The short side remains the ultimate path of low resistance, forcing the defensive line to choose between over-folding to cover a wide threat or leaving a seam open for an immediate halfback snipe. Efficient execution in these congested spaces requires a shift from rigid vertical pod structures toward fluid, system-led principles that prioritize rapid realignment.

#### 2\. KEY PRINCIPLES & CONCEPTS

The current tactical landscape has moved beyond static pod formations like the traditional 1-3-3-1 or 2-4-2. With ruck speeds now averaging 2.9 seconds (LQB), the attack must prioritize "system-led" flexibility over pre-determined channel assignments. This transition ensures that the structure adapts to the defensive picture in real-time rather than following a predictable script.

##### The Symmetrical Advantage

The modern 1-3-3-1 system, utilized effectively by franchises like Connacht, relies on two midfield pods of three forwards. Its primary strength is symmetry, allowing the attack to realign to either side of the pitch instantly without "forcing" the play. By using the second pod as a decoy to draw the midfield defense, playmakers can execute a  **tunnel pass** —throwing behind the pod—to release the fastest players into the wide-blind channels.

##### The "Single Wing" Attack

To neutralize an aggressive Rush defense, we must implement the "Single Wing" or "2-3-3" structure. By over-shifting the forward pack and backs to one side, the offense effectively shortens the playing field to 40 meters or less. This removes the wide passing "intercept windows" that Rush systems target, instead facilitating short "pop" passes and offloads that defuse line-speed.

##### LQB & "The Spares"

The expectation of 0–3 second ball has made rigid vertical channels obsolete. ##### The Exhaust Pattern

This strategy involves a continual attack in one direction, hitting the same defensive seam until the edge is reached or space is identified on the blind side. The "reverse gear" mechanic is the catalyst here; after drawing the defense into a "honeypot" over 3–4 phases, the halfback suddenly reverses the flow to hit a forward on a short-side snipe against a depleted fringe.

#### 3\. PRACTICAL APPLICATION

Modern scoring is dictated by "Launch Plays"—the first 1-3 phases following a set-piece. Analysis of elite competition confirms that 74-75% of tries originate within these first three phases, making the "Third Set Piece" (restarts and dropouts) a critical strike platform.

##### Scrum Base Exploitation

The scrum is our premier platform for blindside manipulation. On "89R" or "89L" calls, the  **No. 9 must tap the No. 8 on the rear**  to signal the break. The No. 8 then picks from the base to draw the opposition flanker, creating a 15-meter corridor to release the 9 and 15 into space.

##### The "Against the Grain" Loop

Synthesizing the "Scotland Scrum Loop" requires a two-wave setup:

1. **Setup:**  The 10, 11, and 12 set up narrow to condense the defense.  
2. **The Pivot:**  The 9 feeds the 12, who carries to the line to attract defenders.  
3. **The Loop:**  The 9 loops back under the 12 to receive a pivot pass. Simultaneously, the 13 runs a hard line  **"against the grain"**  to fix the drift, while the  **No. 10 runs a wide arc**  behind the 13 to bridge the gap to the outside backs.

##### The "Third Set Piece" (Goal-Line Dropouts)

We must treat the goal-line dropout as a strike move. Utilizing the Osborne/Crowley "bluff," our runners must remain  **"Incognito,"**  mimicking the exact pod setup used in previous matches. This exploits "defensive memory," forcing the opposition to over-adjust to a perceived threat while the actual runner penetrates the transitioning line.

##### Short Side Snipes: League vs. Bordeaux Styles

We must choose our modality based on the defensive set. The  **"Rugby League style"**  (Northampton) targets the same defenders repeatedly to force a honeypot. Conversely, the  **"Bordeaux style"**  focuses on wide-to-wide stretching to confuse the defense. Against a set, aggressive line, hitting the same seam (League style) to generate the "reverse gear" snipe is statistically more effective for penetration.

#### 4\. COACHING INSIGHTS

The foundational philosophy is "Trusting the System." Players must be prescriptive in their roles to avoid forcing low-percentage plays.

* **Defensive Manipulation:**  Playmakers must "read the eyes" of the defense. George Ford’s success stems from spotting a 13 dropping into the backfield, signaling an immediate transition from a contestable kick to a short-side carry.  
* **The "Overs Line" Strategy:**  Mandate the use of "overs lines" (running outwards against the drift). As seen with the All Blacks, the runner must  **fade outwards while the ball is in flight** . This catches the defender with their head turned and feet unplanted, sliding the attacker onto the "weak shoulder."  
* **Animation and Threat:**  Support runners must be "animated"—hands up and angling runs—even if they are decoys. This holds the midfield defense and prevents them from sliding to the blind side.  
* **Statistical Reality:**  Because 75% of tries occur in the first 3 phases, multi-phase possession rugby is effectively "dead." We must focus on high-efficiency Launch Plays.

#### 5\. COMMON MISTAKES & HOW TO FIX THEM

Short-side margins are razor-thin. Technical errors here result in immediate turnovers.

* **Poor Alignment (Running Flat):**  Attackers lack the sight-line to adjust.  
* *Fix:*  Mandate depth during the "reload" to ensure runners come onto the ball at pace.  
* **Over-folding:**  Leaving the blind side vacant in anticipation of the open-side shift.  
* *Fix:*  Designate a "spare" to hold the blind-side fringe, keeping the defense honest.  
* **Lack of "Squareness" (Drifting):**  Ball carriers drifting toward touch make the defender's job easy.  
* *Fix:*  Enforce the  **"Outside Foot Up"**  rule. By planting the outside foot to initiate the run, the attacker stays square, fixing the defender and attacking their  **inside shoulder** .

#### 6\. DRILLS & TRAINING PRACTICES

##### Drill 1: Attacking the Drift (5v3)

* **Objective:**  Fix the defense using square alignment.  
* **Success Criteria:**  Attackers must stay square and only turn the chest at the point of release. Use the "Outside Foot Up" rule to ensure straight lines.

##### Drill 2: The Sunlight/Pressure Catch

* **Objective:**  High-ball retrieval in the "dead zone."  
* **Setup:**  Set the kick-chase to land in the  **"Bright Zone"**  (brightest spot on the pitch) to force visual errors from the defender.  
* **Progression:**  Introduce a  **"Two-Ball"**  method. Immediately after the catch, a second ball is introduced to simulate unpredictable, Bordeaux-style offloads, forcing "head-up" vision.

##### Drill 3: The 8-9-15 Blindside Strike

* **Objective:**  Perfect the 8-9-15 timing.  
* **Setup:**  Full scrum. The No. 9 must  **tap the 8 on the rear**  to trigger the pick-up.  
* **Goal:**  The No. 8 draws the flanker to release the backline into the 15m blindside corridor.

#### 7\. KEY TAKEAWAYS

* **Fluidity over Rigidity:**  Abandon static pods; mandate that "Spares" (7, 8, hooker) follow the ball to act as the "Sixth Man" and maintain LQB.  
* **Neutralize the Rush:**  Implement the "Single Wing" (2-3-3) shape to shorten the field and remove intercept windows.  
* **Mandate Squareness:**  Enforce the "Outside Foot Up" rule in all 5m-channel drills to fix defenders on their inside shoulders.  
* **Exploit the Base:**  Use the 8-9 connection at the scrum—triggered by the tactile "rear-tap" cue—to manipulate the blindside fringe.  
* **Prioritize Launch Plays:**  Focus 80% of attacking preparation on the first 3 phases (Launch Plays), as they represent the dominant scoring opportunity in the modern game.  
* **Prescriptive Animation:**  Decoy runners must use "hands up" animation to hold defenders and create short-side seams.
