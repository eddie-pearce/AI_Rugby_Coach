### Technical Report: Decision Making Under Pressure (DMUP) Drills

#### 1\. OVERVIEW

DMUP is the heartbeat of elite performance. It is the ability to process high-velocity defensive and offensive triggers while maintaining technical integrity under extreme physical and temporal stress. Deliberate drill work in this area is the primary differentiator between stagnant player progression and the fluid, dominant transitions required at the breakdown and set piece. These principles constitute the "Coaches Playbook" essential for aligning player development from first contact to the First XV.

#### 2\. KEY PRINCIPLES & CONCEPTS

To achieve technical precision, we must institutionalize these core coaching frameworks:

* **Defensive Choice Architecture:**  
* **The Blitz:**  A proactive system designed to kill the receiver's time. While it disrupts play and forces errors, it leaves the backline susceptible to "cheeky chips" or grubbers; the back three must be triggered to cover this space immediately.  
* **The Drift:**  A containment-based system using the touchline as the 16th defender. It shepherds attackers laterally, making the attack predictable and allowing for side-on leg tackles.  
* **Attack Patterns (1-3-3-1 and 2-4-2):**  
* **1-3-3-1:**  Forwards distribute with one in each 15m channel and two middle pods of three. These pods must operate in a  **"V" formation** , with a backline player standing deeper and directly behind the middle man. This link player is the tactical trigger for the receiver to run, pop, or go "out the back." Calls include  *Squeeze*  (continue direction),  *Bounce*  (switch),  *Flood*  (backdoor to backs), and  *Pierce*  (inside pass).  
* **2-4-2:**  Features two groups of two in wide channels and four in the middle. This allows athletic forwards more room to make things happen—rather than simply catching and running—and ensures ball security with three players at every breakdown.  
* **Ruck Metrics:**  "Quick Ball" is a non-negotiable delivery from ground to hands within 2–3 seconds. Anything exceeding 4 seconds is "Slow Ball," allowing the defense to reorganize and kill attacking momentum.  
* **Safe Engagement:**  The "Crouch, Bind, Set" sequence is the baseline. Technical stability requires "Pinching the Blades" (scapula retraction) and keeping the  **"Chin off chest."**  Looking forward is the only way to prevent spine misalignment.

#### 3\. THE DRILLS

##### Drill 1: Pressure Cooker Passing

* **Objective:**  Develop rapid catch-and-pass accuracy and "traffic" navigation with zero wind-up.  
* **Setup:**  5 players, 3 balls, grid with middle and corner cones.  
* **Execution:**  Carrier moves through the center, taking passes from static middle players and popping them back. They must navigate "visual noise" without losing pace.  
* **Progressions:**  Middle players use flick-passes; increase carrier speed to maximum.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Clean hands, eyes up, and ball moving in one fluid motion without the "wind-up" for power.

##### Drill 2: Passing Grid – Under Pressure (Trigger Recognition)

* **Objective:**  Force immediate realignment and identification of space based on defensive releases.  
* **Setup:**  4 defenders on colored cones (Red/Blue), 4 attackers opposite.  
* **Execution:**  Attackers move the ball. On the coach’s color call, specific defenders release. Attackers must "square up," fix the moving man, and exploit the gap.  
* **Progressions:**  Release two defenders simultaneously; widen the channel to test pass length.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Immediate reaction to the call and early communication from support players to identify the hole.

##### Drill 3: The Decision-Making Channel (Numbered Cones)

* **Objective:**  Train vision and anticipation against variable defensive numbers.  
* **Setup:**  Channel with 5 attackers; defenders on numbered cones.  
* **Execution:**  Coach calls numbers (e.g., "4, 1") to release defenders mid-play. Attackers must scan the line mid-run and adjust depth.  
* **Progressions:**  Call multiple defenders from a single cone to create a "thick" defensive point.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Attackers identifying the defensive shape early and adjusting the line’s depth to maintain the overlap.

##### Drill 4: Defensive Trigger Game (Blitz vs. Drift)

* **Objective:**  Unit autonomy in reading "Hold" vs. "Go" triggers.  
* **Setup:**  Full defensive line vs. attacking unit.  
* **Execution:**  Line identifies triggers. "Slow Ball" or "Flat Receivers" trigger the Blitz ("Up\!"). "Quick Ball" or "Deep Playmakers" trigger the Drift ("Hold/Push\!").  
* **Progressions:**  Remove coach calls; the Captain makes all "Up\!" or "Hold\!" calls based on the attacking shape.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  A connected line moving as one unit. Zero "dog-legs." Back three covering the space behind the Blitz.

##### Drill 5: The "Hook and Handle" Ruck Clear-Out

* **Objective:**  Legal, effective removal of the "Jackal" to ensure 2-second ball.  
* **Setup:**  Ball carrier, tackler, and first arrival support.  
* **Execution:**   **Enter through the gate**  and  **stay on your feet** . Arrive low, hook the defender’s legs, and lift. You are removing their "base"—they cannot resist when their feet are off the ground. Drive them entirely past the ball.  
* **Progressions:**  Increase Jackal resistance; add a second cleaner to work as a pod.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Jaws below hips, flat back, and the defender’s feet leaving the turf as they are driven off the base.

#### 4\. COACHING INSIGHTS

* **The Power of Silence:**  Command the room. Speak only when everyone is quiet. Silence lowers the "temporal stress" for players, allowing them to absorb technical triggers.  
* **Intensity Management:**  Prime the nervous system. Move from "Tiger Tails" to full-speed simulations. Never coach technical precision at walking pace once the baseline is set.  
* **Triggers over Rules:**  Coach the  *vision* . Don't just tell them where to run; tell them to watch the ball carrier's hips. Reading the playmaker’s depth is the key to the defensive system.  
* **Technical Shape over Power:**  In the scrum, technique solves "troublesome moments" that raw power cannot.  **"Chin off chest"**  and hips facing forward are the only ways to stay square and safe.

#### 7\. KEY TAKEAWAYS

* **Ball Presentation is Non-Negotiable:**  The carrier wins the ruck by placing the ball far from defenders. Immediate release is the baseline for the 2–3 second "Quick Ball" metric.  
* **Communicate or Fail:**  A defensive line is only as strong as its loudest communicator. "Up\!" and "Hold\!" calls must be instantaneous and followed by the whole unit.  
* **Remove the Base:**  Use the "Hook and Handle" to lift the Jackal's feet off the ground. If they have no base, they have no contest.  
* **Read the Link:**  In the 1-3-3-1, the depth of the backline player behind the middle man in the "V" formation is the primary trigger for the attack's direction.  
* **Scrum Safety is Performance:**  "Chin off chest" and "Pinching the blades" are not just safety cues; they create the rigid, stable platform required to win the set piece.  
* **Blitz Awareness:**  When triggered to Blitz, the back three must immediately drop to cover the space behind the line to negate chips and grubbers.
