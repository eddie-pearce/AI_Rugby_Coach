### Strike Move & Set Play Rehearsal Drills Report

##### 1\. OVERVIEW

The strategic rehearsal of set plays and strike moves serves as the vital bridge between isolated technical proficiency and cohesive tactical execution. In the elite environment, success is predicated on a team’s ability to transition seamlessly from the static structures of scrums and lineouts into dynamic, high-tempo attack shapes without a breakdown in momentum. This report analyzes the rehearsal of core components—including specialized forward pods (1-3-3-1 and 2-4-2) and set-piece platforms—critical for maintaining scoreboard pressure. Deliberate "rehearsal" develops the technical tightness and unit synchronization required to perform under match-day duress, whereas "random play" frequently results in structural decay and lost gain-line advantage. Ultimately, mastering these choreographed sequences ensures that collective execution becomes an instinctive response to the shifting defensive landscapes of the modern game.

##### 2\. KEY PRINCIPLES & CONCEPTS

Technical execution is effectively worthless without a rigorous conceptual framework. Players must move beyond rote memorization to recognize tactical "triggers" that dictate system transitions.

* **1-3-3-1 Attack Formation:**  A system where the forward pack is distributed across the field width: one forward in each 15-meter channel (typically the flankers or 8\) and two pods of three forwards operating in the central zone. Link players (usually the 10 and 12\) sit between these pods to facilitate ball movement.  
* **2-4-2 Attack Formation:**  This utilizes two pods of two players in the wide 15-meter channels (hooker/flanker/8 and wingers) and a central "working pod" of four (props and locks). This spreads the defense and allows forwards more room to make plays, ensuring ball security with a consistent three-player presence at the breakdown.  
* **Blitz Defense:**  An aggressive disruption philosophy where the line shoots up in unison on the trigger of the pass to force errors and recycle ball behind the gain line. Success relies on synchronized timing; one player out of sync creates a fatal gap.  
* **Drift Defense:**  A containment strategy using the touchline as the "16th defender." Players move laterally, watching the ball carrier’s hips to shepherd the attack wide and nullify overlaps through patience rather than collision.  
* **Breakdown Standards:**  Elite "Quick Ball" is measured by a 2–3 second metric from ground-hit to scrum-half clearance. The "Hook and Handle" is the prioritized clear-out technique: arriving low, hooking the defender's legs to remove their base, and driving them past the ball.  
* **The "Trigger" Concept:**  Cues that dictate system switches. For example, slow ball or a static receiver triggers a "Blitz," while a playmaker standing deep or a quick-ball scenario signals a "Hold" or "Drift."

##### 3\. THE DRILLS: SET PIECE & STRIKE ATTACK

Set-piece development must prioritize "technical tightness" and safety. Following Graham Rowntree’s principles, coaching focuses on "positive profiling" and stability, ensuring players can hook and strike effectively without compromising their spine or core.

###### *Category A: Set Piece (Scrum & Lineout)*

**Drill 1: 1v1 Scrum (On Knees/Feet)**

* **Objective:**  Validate binding integrity and establish "positive profiling" for core stability.  
* **Setup:**  Two players facing each other; progress from knees to feet.  
* **Execution:**  Engage using "Crouch, Bind, Set." On "Set," players sink from the hips. Bind specifically between the opponent's armpit and the shoulder blade. Second rows must "kiss your elbow" to maintain a low, tight bind and prevent pulling.  
* **Progressions:**  Elite progression: "Shuffle two steps and hold." This tests stability and "compliance" during lateral movement.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Chins off chests; "hollowed" back (imagine pinching fingers between shoulder blades); hips below chest; players staying "in their own lane."**Drill 2: Piston Jumping**  
* **Objective:**  Synthesize pod movement with timed lifting mechanics and coordination.  
* **Setup:**  Two or more lineout pods standing 3–5 meters apart.  
* **Execution:**  Pod A performs a lift and hold. The jumper stays in the air until Pod B reaches the apex of their jump. The ball is then lobbed to Jumper B.  
* **Progressions:**  Lifters take two steps laterally while the jumper is in the air to test core activation and base stability.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Lifters driving chests forward into the hold; jumper securing the ball at the highest point with two hands.**Drill 3: Lineout Race**  
* **Objective:**  Validate accuracy and pod speed under conditions of high fatigue.  
* **Setup:**  Two teams of four (1 thrower, 3 pod players) lined up 5–10 meters apart.  
* **Execution:**  Teams race across the pitch. At each station, the thrower must hit the jumper. The pod moves only after a successful catch and ground.  
* **Progressions:**  Add a "defensive rotator" to shadow pods and attempt interceptions.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Clean transition between spots; immediate arm extension to secure the ball before the defense can react.

###### *Category B: Strike Moves & Attack Shape*

**Drill 4: 4 vs 3 Team Shapes**

* **Objective:**  Synthesize pod movement with decision-making against reduced defensive numbers.  
* **Setup:**  Simulated ruck on the back foot. 4 attackers vs. 3 defenders. Defenders must stay on the "back foot" to simulate realistic line speed.  
* **Execution:**  Attackers execute pod shapes (1-3-3-1 or 2-4-2) against the defense. Upon completion, they immediately turn and attack back to the original spot.  
* **Progressions:**  Introduce a "blindside winger" roaming into the 15m channel as an extra option.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Clinical identification of the man-advantage; wingers hitting the 15m channel at pace.**Drill 5: L-Shaped Passing**  
* **Objective:**  Validate lateral ball flow and passing accuracy at high tempo.  
* **Setup:**  Players in an "L" formation; one ball starting at the end of the line.  
* **Execution:**  Move forward, passing down the line. Last receiver passes to the first receiver of the next waiting line.  
* **Progressions:**  Increase pace to "Fast Hands" metrics; use "no-look passes" to test support player depth.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Catching with hands out in front of the face; "passing through the feet" to transfer weight for maximum power and accuracy.**Drill 6: MT 1-3-3-1 Pattern Simulation**  
* **Objective:**  Synthesize forward pack movement with specific tactical calls.  
* **Setup:**  Full pack (blue) and link backs (red) distributed across the pitch.  
* **Execution:**  Movement based on unit calls.  
* **"Squeeze":**  Continue in the same direction for two consecutive phases.  
* **"Bounce":**  Switch direction by moving a pod back the way it came.  
* **"Flood":**  Backdoor option to 12/13. Crucially, the associated flanker/8 and winger must flood up together in a flat, urgent line.  
* **"Pierce":**  An inside pass from the 10 to the 15 to break the gain line.  
* **Progressions:**  Transition from coach-led calls to the 9/10 reading defensive triggers.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Pods moving flexibly; flat, urgent lines on "Flood" and "Pierce" executions.

###### *Category C: Defensive Rehearsal*

**Drill 7: Blitz vs. Drift Decision Grid**

* **Objective:**  Validate unit trigger recognition and synchronized line speed.  
* **Setup:**  Defensive line vs. attacking unit.  
* **Execution:**  Coach calls "Blitz" or "Drift" last second. Defense adjusts spacing and speed. Players must utilize adjustment calls: "Inside\!", "Numbers\!", or "Weak\!" to reset the line.  
* **Progressions:**  Remove coach calls; defenders must read triggers (e.g., playmaker depth) to decide the system.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Total "compliance" with the call; line moving as a unified wall; zero gaps between defenders.

##### 4\. COACHING INSIGHTS: FROM GOOD TO GREAT

The "Art of Coaching" is defined by the clarity and timing of information. Elite rehearsal requires the coach to assert match-day standards through "Active Refereeing."

* **Communication Standards:**  Utilize the "Silence Rule." Only deliver instructions when the group is entirely quiet. Ask for silence, wait for focus, and only then explain. This ensures 100% absorption of technical cues.  
* **Trigger Coaching:**  Transition from coach-directed drills to player-led decision-making. Use "hinge questions" (e.g., "What cue did the 10 give that triggered the Drift?") to validate player understanding.  
* **Refereeing Actively:**  Assert match laws during every rep. Specifically, enforce "gate" compliance, entry angles, and the 5-second ball release rule. Rehearsal without law compliance creates "penalty-prone" habits.

##### 6\. SESSION DESIGN: THE 60-MINUTE REHEARSAL BLOCK

Using the "Whole-Part-Whole" methodology, sessions begin with a game to establish tactical context, move to isolated skill refinement, and conclude with high-intensity simulation.

1. **Opening (1 min):**  Health/Safety (checks for jewelry/watches) and "Opening Lines" to set the performance tone.  
2. **Warm-up Game (15 min):**  High-tempo scanning games (e.g., "Snap, Crackle, and Pop" or "Go To Goal Touch").  
3. **Skill 1: Technical Set Piece (12 min):**  Focus on 1v1 profiling, "Kiss your elbow" binds, and technical tightness.  
4. **Skill 2: Tactical Shape (12 min):**  Pod movements (1-3-3-1/2-4-2). Focus on the transition from set-piece to pod formation (e.g., props/locks moving to the "working pod").  
5. **Game Simulation (20 min):**  Touch variation (e.g., "Four Handed Touch") enforcing "Quick Ball" metrics and active refereeing on breakdown entry.

##### 7\. KEY TAKEAWAYS

* **Compliance is Success:**  In the elite scrum, victory is defined by compliance—not pushing early and maintaining legal binds (between armpit and shoulder blade).  
* **Prioritize Ball Presentation:**  Presentation wins rucks. Prioritize clean placement over extra yardage to maintain a \<3 second ruck speed.  
* **The "Hook and Handle":**  Efficiency is found in removing the defender’s base. Lifting is more effective and legally sustainable than a straight drive.  
* **Synchronized Line Speed:**  One defender out of sync destroys the Blitz. Communication ("Inside\!", "Numbers\!") is a non-negotiable requirement.  
* **Core Foundation:**  Stability in the scrum is generated by forward-facing hips and body height (hips below chest), not raw pushing power.  
* **Silence for Clarity:**  Effective coaching requires a silent audience. Do not compete with player noise; wait for total focus before delivering cues.
