### Technical Report: Lineout Jumping, Lifting & Calling Drills

##### 1\. OVERVIEW

The lineout is a non-negotiable battleground for territorial dominance; we demand absolute technical precision to secure a clean attacking platform or disrupt the opposition’s possession. This report mandates specific standards for jumping, lifting, and calling to ensure our set-piece remains a weapon of tactical dominance under match-day pressure. Elite performance is predicated on individual technical proficiency, which provides the bedrock for the sophisticated unit-work detailed in our coaching frameworks.

##### 2\. KEY PRINCIPLES & CONCEPTS

To establish a dominant unit, we utilize a structured approach to physical activation and synchronization. The following frameworks are mandatory:

* **Core Activation:**  We utilize "Bungie" and "Swiss Ball" methodologies to achieve "positive profiling." Players must pull the core in to flatten the back while simultaneously firing the glutes forward and squeezing them tight. This creates a rigid, aerodynamic column that is easier to lift and stabilize.  
* **Pod Synchronization:**  We employ the "Piston" concept for timing, ensuring that as one jumper hits the peak, the next is already ascending. This is governed by the "Full Lifts Process," mandating that all three players in the pod move as a single, cohesive unit.  
* **Technical Loading:**  Non-negotiable hand placement is required: the back lifter secures a grip high on the back of the legs, while the front lifter binds just above the knee. Lifters must "sink from the hips" with a flat back and execute an "explosive leg drive," pushing their "chests forward" and "wrapping inside arms" tight around the jumper's shorts or T-shirt to ensure a locked, secure bind.**"So What?" Analysis:**  Failure to maintain these standards leads to "soft core" instability. Beyond the physical loss of the ball, a jumper who drifts or buckles creates an "unstable" image for the referee. In elite rugby, instability is often interpreted as a technical foul or a safety violation, leading to immediate penalties. Strict glute firing and "kissing the elbow" during the bind ensure the center of mass remains vertical, ensuring both possession and referee compliance.These physical principles must be applied through high-intensity, directive drill work.

##### 3\. THE DRILLS: TECHNICAL FOUNDATIONS & EXECUTION

###### *Piston Jumping*

* **Objective:**  Develops isometric hold strength, endurance at the peak, and synchronized unit timing.  
* **Setup:**  Two pods (3 players each) in a line with one ball.  
* **Execution:**  Use the cadence  **"Crouch, Bind, Set."**  On "Set," Pod A lifts their jumper.  **Mandatory:**  Pod A must hold the jumper at the peak for 3 seconds to develop isometric conditioning. Pod B then initiates their lift on the coach's whistle. As Jumper B reaches the peak, Jumper A lobs the ball to them. Pod A returns to the ground only after the catch is secured.  
* **Progressions:**  Introduce a "Reaction" element where the coach points to a pod to initiate the next "piston" stroke.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Constant vertical extension, no dipping of the hips, and elbows "kissed" tight to the body.

###### *Lineout Race*

* **Objective:**  To drill speed of setup and technical accuracy under extreme fatigue.  
* **Setup:**  Two pods (4 players including thrower) 5–10 meters apart across the pitch.  
* **Execution:**  On the command "GO," pods sprint across the pitch. At the designated mark, the front lifter turns to face the pod. The thrower delivers the ball as the pod lifts using the  **"Crouch, Bind, Set"**  trigger.  **Consequence:**  If the ball is dropped, the throw MUST be taken again from the same position before the pod can advance.  
* **Progressions:**  Add a requirement to deliver an "off the top" pass to a roving scrum-half before moving to the next station.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Immediate transition from sprint to a "sink from the hips" stance; simultaneous drive.

###### *3-Pod Lifting (Off the Top)*

* **Objective:**  Accuracy of the "off the top" pass and maintenance of a strong unit base.  
* **Setup:**  3 pods of 3 men arranged in a triangle, 3 meters apart.  
* **Execution:**  Follow the  **"Crouch, Bind, Set"**  timing for the initial lift. The thrower hits Pod 1; the jumper catches and immediately delivers a flat pass "off the top" to a lifter in Pod 2\. The ball is passed around the triangle while pods maintain their isometric holds.  
* **Progressions:**  Increase distance between pods or add a "Bluff" call where one pod jumps without a ball.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  High hands on the catch; minimal "drift" in the air; chests driven forward into the hold.

###### *Reaction Lifts*

* **Objective:**  Develops reactive unit communication and explosive upward drive.  
* **Setup:**  Pods of three using tackle bags to simulate jumper weight.  
* **Execution:**  Use  **"Crouch, Bind, Set"**  timing. On the coach’s call of "Left" or "Right," lifters must react, turn, and drive the bag upward.  
* **Progressions:**  Transition from static bags to a live jumper moving  **dynamically between cones** . The lifters must track the jumper’s movement and "Set" only when the jumper is square.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Hips facing forward during the drive; "positive profiling" with flat backs.

###### *Lineout Lift \+ Balance*

* **Objective:**  Core stability and handling skills in a sustained, moving hold.  
* **Setup:**  Groups of 3\.  
* **Execution:**  Pod initiates lift. Once at the peak, the lifters take 3 small, controlled steps ("Squeeze") to the side while maintaining the jumper’s verticality.  
* **Progressions:**  The coach throws balls at various heights/angles to test the jumper’s ability to remain rigid while moving.  
* **What Good Looks Like:**  Sustained extension; no "noodle" core; feet moving in unison.These drills build the physical capacity and cohesion required to implement elite strategic insights.

##### 4\. COACHING INSIGHTS: FROM GOOD TO GREAT

Strategic deception is worthless if the pod's hand placement allows the jumper to drift. We must layer tactical sophistication over our technical base.**Calling & Deception**  Elite units use "Bluffs" and "Space Interceptors." The  **Bluff**  requires varied formations—such as putting the hooker in the 5m channel—to unnerve the opposition. The  **Space Interceptor**  is an aggressive defensive tactic. We do not shadow the attacker; we jump into the space  *in front*  of them.  **Micro-cue:**  The jumper’s inside arm must be up immediately to intercept the throw. This mechanic is vital to ensure the referee sees a "play for the ball" rather than "playing the man."**The "One Thought" Rule**  Non-negotiable: We only speak when everyone is quiet. Wait for total silence, then deliver a singular coaching thought (e.g., "Hips forward"). This ensures technical cues land and stick during high-intensity work.**Referee Compliance**  Stability equals compliance. We use the "Scrum Factory" principle of "kissing the elbow"—pulling the binding arm tight—to keep the pod "down" and compact. A stable, technical unit that returns the jumper safely to the ground earns the referee's trust, particularly during the transition to the maul.

##### 5\. COMMON MISTAKES & CORRECTIVE INTERVENTIONS

Common Error,Root Cause,Corrective Intervention  
Poor Body Shape,"Hips below chests; ""chin on chest"" leading to arched spine.","""Positive Profiling"": Mandate ""chins off chest."" Use the  ""ball on back""  check to ensure a flat, hollowed profile."  
Desynchronized Lifts,"Lack of a shared ""trigger"" or one lifter driving early.","Revert to strict  ""Crouch, Bind, Set""  cadence. Focus on the ""simultaneous drive"" in Piston drills."  
Soft Core/Instability,"Failing to ""pull the core in"" or fire glutes; ""noodle"" jumper.","Immediate return to Swiss Ball/Bungie activation. Cue: ""Squeeze glutes at extension."""  
Poor Hand Placement,"Gripping too low; failing to ""kiss the elbow"" on the bind.","Front lifter: ""Above the knee."" Back lifter: ""High on back of legs."" Drive ""chests forward."""

##### 6\. SESSION DESIGN: THE 60-MINUTE BLUEPRINT

This session integrates technical standards into match-pressure scenarios using the 1-15-12-12-20 framework.

1. **Warm-Up: Agility & Scanning (15 mins):**  Play "Tomb Raider." Cues: Constant scanning for space, high-tempo agility, and immediate awareness of ball placement.  
2. **Skill Block 1: Activation & Form (12 mins):**  Focus on Bungie/Swiss Ball activation. Drill "Scrum Profile" and static lifting. Mandate "positive profiling" and flat backs.  
3. **Skill Block 2: Unit Competition (12 mins):**  Execute "Lineout Race." Implement the consequence: any drop results in a restart from that spot. Pressure technical hand placement under fatigue.  
4. **Game Simulation: Line-Out Only Restart (20 mins):**  Every reset begins with a lineout (1 thrower, 2 catchers, 4 backs). Apply "Bluff" and "Space Interceptor" tactics.Success is measured by the long-term retention of these standards through repetitive, high-quality execution.

##### 7\. KEY TAKEAWAYS

* **Precision Over Power:**  Positive profiling—flat backs and chins off chests—is the mandatory standard for stability.  
* **Mandatory Core Activation:**  Glute firing and "pulling the core in" are non-negotiable precursors to every session.  
* **Synchronization is Supreme:**  A desynchronized lift is a failed lift. Use the "Crouch, Bind, Set" trigger for every repetition.  
* **Deception Through Variation:**  Disrupt the opposition’s rhythm using "Bluffs" and varied formations.  
* **Communication Excellence:**  Adhere to the "One Thought Rule"—speak only when the group is quiet.  
* **Referee Compliance:**  Stability and "kissing the elbow" ensure safety and technical approval from the official.
