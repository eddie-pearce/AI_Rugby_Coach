### Technical Report: Ball Carrying, Contact Entry, and Breakdown Drills

##### 1\. OVERVIEW

This report defines the technical standards for ball carrying, the mechanics of contact entry, and the tactical framework of the breakdown. Mastering the collision zone is the non-negotiable foundation for maintaining attacking momentum and generating "quick ball"—the specific 2–3 second delivery threshold where defensive "folding" and reorganization break down. Technical proficiency in these areas is not a goal but a prerequisite; without elite execution at the point of contact, the advanced team shapes and tactical principles detailed in this document cannot be sustained under match pressure.

##### 2\. KEY PRINCIPLES & CONCEPTS

Commanding a game requires a unified coaching framework where every player operates from the same technical script. Strategic success is a byproduct of standardized terminology and the ruthless execution of the following core concepts:

* **The Contact Interface**  
* **"Gate" Entry:**  Support players must enter the ruck from behind the hindmost foot of the teammate on the ground to remain legal and effective.  
* **"Hook and Handle" Clear-out:**  A superior technical alternative to the straight drive. Players arrive low, hook under the defender’s legs, and lift their weight to remove their base, driving them beyond the ball.  
* **The "Jackal":**  A high-risk defensive contest where the player attacks the ball immediately after a tackle, maintaining a strong, low base to rip the ball or force a penalty before the ruck forms.  
* **Ball Presentation Mechanics**  
* **The "Long Placement" Principle:**  Upon ground contact, the carrier must place the ball as far from the defender as possible using both hands with arms extended back toward support.  
* **Body Shielding:**  The carrier must use their body as a physical shield between the opposition and the ball, ensuring immediate release to avoid "sealing off" or turnover penalties.  
* **Offensive Structures**  
* **1-3-3-1 Formation:**  Forwards are distributed with one "edge" player in each 15m channel and two pods of three in the middle.  
* **Tactical Calls:**   **"Squeeze"**  (Pods continue in the same direction for 2 phases),  **"Bounce"**  (Switch direction of play),  **"Flood"**  (Backdoor option to 12/13 with the edge forward and wing), and  **"Pierce"**  (Inside pass from 10 to 15 to break the gain line).  
* **2-4-2 Formation:**  Pairs of forwards occupy the 15m channels with a central block of four. This prioritizes ball security and allows forwards more space to act as playmakers.  
* **Defensive Decision-Making**  
* **The Blitz:**  An aggressive system designed to disrupt the carrier and force recycling behind the gain line. It is triggered by  **slow ball** ,  **static receivers** , or the  **scrum-half taking extra time** .  
* **The Drift:**  A containment system using the touchline as the "16th defender." It is utilized against quick ball or when defenders lack explosive pace. Players must watch the  **carrier’s hips, not the ball** , to avoid being beaten by decoys.These theoretical principles provide the blueprint, but they must be stress-tested through deliberate drill progressions to translate into match-day performance.

##### 3\. THE DRILLS: EXECUTION AND APPLICATION

To bridge the gap between skill acquisition and match-day intuition, drills must move from "closed" mechanical work to "open" decision-making. The transition from static technical cues to high-pressure environments is where elite habits are forged.

###### *Drill 1: Diamond Shape Supporting Lines (Continuity)*

* **Objective:**  Developing support lines and offload timing to maintain attacking continuity.  
* **Setup:**  Rectangular area; 4 attackers in a diamond; 3 defenders stacked diagonally.  
* **Execution:**  The ball carrier is tackled and must offload to a supporting attacker. As the carrier is "grounded," the support must immediately tighten into an  **arrow formation**  to protect the ball and maintain the point of the spear.  
* **Progressions:**  Increase defensive line speed; require offloads before the knee hits the ground.  
* **"What Good Looks Like":**  Support players reacting instantly to the carrier’s grounding; support tightening into the arrow to maintain continuity.

###### *Drill 2: Fixing Defender Progression (Sausage/Bag Work)*

* **Objective:**  Using footwork to "fix" a defender and create inside passing lanes.  
* **Setup:**  Channels with tackle "sausages"  **lined up one behind the other**  in a row; groups of 5\.  
* **Execution:**  Attackers must use footwork to step to one side of the sausage, squaring up their hips to the "defender" before releasing the pass to a support player.  
* **Progressions:**  Move from static bags to "Pass Invaders" (moving defenders with bags).  
* **"What Good Looks Like":**  Attackers "squaring up" the defender; passing before contact with the bag; explosive footwork between sausages.

###### *Drill 3: One Touch Ruck (Ball Retention)*

* **Objective:**  Sharpening ruck entry speed and ball presentation under fatigue.  
* **Setup:**  Standard grid; two teams in bibs.  
* **Execution:**  Upon being touched, the carrier goes to ground and presents. Two teammates must "ruck over," staying on their feet to provide an "armchair ride" for the scrum-half.  
* **Progressions:**  Defense must commit three players to every ruck to increase pressure.  
* **"What Good Looks Like":**  Immediate ball release upon ground contact; cleaners driving past the ball through "the gate."

###### *Drill 4: Snap, Crackle, and POP (Contact Offloads)*

* **Objective:**  Driving through contact to identify "the hole" and offload.  
* **Setup:**  Split players into two teams; touch-rugby style.  
* **Execution:**  When touched, the carrier goes to ground and must "pop" the ball to a support player immediately.  **Rule: The defender who made the touch cannot make the next touch.**  This forces the receiver to drive past the original tackler.  
* **Progressions:**  Turnover the ball for any delay in the pop pass.  
* **"What Good Looks Like":**  Rapid transition from floor to support; receiver identifying the "hole" created by the sidelined defender.

###### *Drill 5: 1v1 Scrum Profile (Knees and Feet Stability)*

* **Objective:**  Developing a safe, powerful individual body position.  
* **Setup:**  1v1 pairs.  
* **Execution:**  Players sink from the hips.  
* **Cues:**  Command the players to  **"pinch my fingers"**  between their shoulder blades to set the back and  **"kiss your elbow"**  (for second rows) to keep the binding low and tight.  
* **Progressions:**  Move from knees to feet; execute a "shuffle" of two steps while maintaining the profile.  
* **"What Good Looks Like":**  A flat, neutral spine; hips facing forward; chins off chests.

###### *Drill 6: Piston Jumping (Lineout Continuity)*

* **Objective:**  Timing and lifting stability for aerial ball transfer.  
* **Setup:**  Two lineout pods (jumper and two lifters).  
* **Execution:**  Pod A lifts and holds. As Pod B lifts, the ball is lobbed from Jumper A to Jumper B. Lifters must  **"drive chests forward"**  into the hold for stability.  
* **Progressions:**  Lifters take small side-steps while the jumper is in the air to test the base.  
* **"What Good Looks Like":**  Jumpers held at the peak; chests driven forward by lifters; accurate "off the top" transfers.Drill success is only realized when the coach applies the nuanced insights of delivery to ensure technical cues are internalized.

##### 4\. COACHING INSIGHTS: ELEVATING THE SESSION

The "Art of Coaching" dictates that the delivery of information is as vital as the content. Technical expertise is useless if the message is lost in a chaotic environment.

* **The Power of Silence:**  Command total silence before giving a technical directive. Wait for the group to settle, ask again, and only speak when you have 100% attention. This ensures the "one coaching thought" of the segment sticks.  
* **The "Whole-Part-Whole" Methodology:**  Use this to bridge the gap between drill and game. Start with a game (Whole), break out into technical skill blocks (Part), and return to the game (Whole) to validate if the technical cues are applied under pressure.  
* **Intensity vs. Duration:**  High quality over high volume. Limit first sessions to 60 minutes. Target 2–3 second ruck speeds consistently to simulate the neurological demands of match play.  
* **Feedback Loops:**  Utilize  **"Hinge Questions"**  to verify understanding. Instead of "Do you understand?", ask "If the defense is folding slowly, what is our Squeeze trigger?"The feedback loops established in this phase are the essential tools used to deliver the corrective interventions required when technique breaks down.

##### 5\. COMMON MISTAKES & CORRECTIVE INTERVENTIONS

Bad habits in the contact zone lead to turnovers, penalties, and defensive "folding." ##### 6\. SESSION DESIGN: THE INTEGRATED TRAINING PLAN

Effective sessions utilize  **Tactical Periodization** , aligning the physical workload with the tactical theme. The high-intensity agility of the warm-up prepares the Central Nervous System (CNS) for the explosive technical work in the contact blocks.

* **Phase 1: Warm-up (15 mins):**  High-energy games like "Tiger Tails" or "Escape the Square" to prime scanning and agility.  
* **Phase 2: Skill Block 1 (12 mins):**  Technical focus on ball carrying and placement (Diamond Shape).  
* **Phase 3: Skill Block 2 (12 mins):**  Transition to contact entry and clear-outs. Focus on "Hook and Handle" and Scrum Profiling (pinch blades/kiss elbow cues).  
* **Phase 4: Game Simulation (20 mins):**  Small-sided games (Touch Ruck/Living on the Edge). Apply 1-3-3-1 or 2-4-2 shapes, focusing on "Squeeze" or "Bounce" calls.  
* **Phase 5: Cool-down & Review (1 min):**  Direct chat to reinforce the "One Coaching Thought" of the day.

##### 7\. KEY TAKEAWAYS

* **The 2-3 Second Standard:**  Ruck speed is the primary metric of success. Anything slower allows the Blitz to reset and the defense to fold.  
* **Master the "Hook and Handle":**  Lifting the defender's base is the most efficient way to remove them from the contest while remaining on your feet.  
* **Tactical Communication:**  Commands like "Squeeze," "Bounce," and "Flood" must be non-negotiable within the 1-3-3-1 to ensure pod flexibility.  
* **Scrum Profile Safety:**  Technical excellence (flat back, "pinch fingers") is the only way to ensure both dominant power and player safety.  
* **Drift Defense Discipline:**  Defenders must watch the carrier's  **hips, not the ball** , to prevent being "stepped" or deceived by decoy runners.  
* **Coaching Flow:**  Use the Whole-Part-Whole method to ensure that technical skills are never divorced from the tactical reality of the game.
